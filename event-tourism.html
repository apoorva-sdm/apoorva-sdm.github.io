OVERVIEW

India’s Travel & Tourism sector contributes roughly 6.6% of national GDP and is projected to reach $527 billion by 2036. Building on this momentum, we undertook a comprehensive research journey to uncover insights that could shape India’s rise as a global live events hub. The ambition is set out in the official frameworks and execution roadmaps of the Live Events Development Cell (LEDC), a body established under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (MIB), Government of India. Its target is to position India as a leading live events destination by 2030.

CHALLENGE

How might we turn India’s existing tourism strength into a world-class live events offering that draws international artists, organizers, and audiences, and makes the country a first-choice destination for events?

Championing Gujarat for Indian Event Tourism

India is the world's largest living laboratory for mass cultural events yet sits outside the global top 5 event tourism destinations. The deficit is not cultural. It is structural. No unified framework exists for curation, authenticity management, or experience design at scale.

Gujarat is the Land of Legends, and is also one of India’s densest festival landscapes, with around 3,500 fairs and festivals celebrated across the state every year with its flagship events expecting a footfall of 7L to 15L and growing without any way to measure whether their culture is holding up.

ESSENCE SPHERES

The key terms of this project are its seven essence spheres: Culture, Authenticity, Experience, Narrative, Shared Identity, Legacy, and Holistic Growth and together these interlinked dimensions define what makes an event indigenous. Click on a term to understand it better.

Culture

The shared way of life of a community, rooted in religion, belief, tradition, and legacy, expressed through language, ritual, food, art, music, dance, clothing, craft, and theatre. Fluid — continuously reinterpreted across generations. Not a product. Not a performance.

Experience

Time-bound, place-specific gatherings that surround attendees with layered sensory experience — stirring emotions from joy to apprehension — allowing people to engage as observers, participants, or active co-creators.

Authenticity

When a community's evolving way of life is seen, received, and engaged with on that community's own terms, without displacing their authority over how it is interpreted. Culture + Validity = Authenticity.

Legacy

The bidirectional ethos belonging to both a person and their community simultaneously. Cannot be fully owned by either. The condition that makes a visitor feel they belong, not that they are watching.

Shared Identity

The bidirectional ethos belonging to both a person and their community simultaneously. Cannot be fully owned by either. The condition that makes a visitor feel they belong, not that they are watching.

Narrative

The single credible central theme that governs what gets included and what does not. For the consumer: an experience strong enough to travel for and share by word of mouth.

Holistic Growth

Built through genuine sensory experiences people did not expect but will not forget. Every part of the event expands without losing the original reason it existed.

INFERENTIAL DATA ANALYSIS

The Inferential Analysis of the coded descriptions, revealed major and minor patterns that were emerging. In accordance with the Pareto’s 80-20 rule, 20% of the root causes were leading to 80% of the issues in the event tourism ecosystem with respect to the fairs and festivals of Gujarat

Major Patterns

Minor Patterns

Events which are centred or created around local communities/crowd not only empower them but also ensure an authentic, safe, and unique experience for the attendees, creating curiosity for the crowd.

Events must be resonating and evolving with respect to their potential visitors and visitors' mindsets while retaining its core and authentic essence.

Due to the prior notion of completing checklists/wishlists, people end up not experiencing the culture and it leads to lack of civic sense, local resistance, and frustration for the visitors/attendees.

Event promotion must be balanced to maintain visitor's curiosity, awareness and over promotion.

Events should create a strong identity narrative through culture/heritage to create an authentic experience for the visitors and to let them know why they are there.

Event organizers are unable to promote the original USP of the event due to low co-ordination between departments and gaps in knowledge.

Footfall being the strongest, sometimes the only metric for success, event managers have become more competitive rather than collaborating with each other.

Due to migration and commercialisation, artists, artisans and craft villages are forced to upskill by abandoning their culture through contemporary methods.

Over-promotion can lead to dilution of authenticity of the event. This could possibly lead to overcrowding, devaluing culture, leading to frustration in fellow visitors.

INSIGHTS

1

Cultural commoditization is resulting in pseudo-authenticity of the fairs and festivals of Gujarat.

2

Absence of a cultural essence framework results in ignorance among stakeholders, and dilution of culture in Gujarat's fairs and festivals.

3

Gujarat's fair and festival's sole success metric is footfall, resulting in fake authenticity winning over real depth.

4

FOMO driven checklist behaviour due to passive participation and civic disengagement results in event attendance solely for social validation.

5

Art-form evolution is the only continuous, compounding outcome in events, but remains untracked, despite sustained legacy and future public investment.

6

Economic inviability of local communities results in their reduced participation and cultural inauthenticity.

DESIGN DIRECTION

Authenticity Tracker is a novel time-series tool that measures whether an event is moving toward cultural and authentic evolution or dilution based on 20 success dimensions developed through secondary research. The solution depicts the final score for a major Gujarat event such as Rann Utsav based on the complete 20 success dimensions including 7 essence dimensions.

Refer to the 07 essence dimensions

Pick 01 out of 07 dimensions to work on

For each essence dimension, refer to the critical success factors

Based on the Likert scale, and data collected,assign a number from 0 - 10 for each critical sucess factors

Map the spider chart after assigning a score to all critical success factors and averaging them to get a single score per dimension

Repeat this same procedure for remaining 06 dimensions

Plot all the scores for each dimension on a new spider chart and average them to get either score <= 5 or >5
